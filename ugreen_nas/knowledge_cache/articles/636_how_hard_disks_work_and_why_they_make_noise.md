# How Hard Disks Work and Why They Make Noise

### **I****、Working Principles of Hard Disks and Noise Generation Mechanism**

1.**Core Structure of a Mechanical Hard Disk (HDD)**  A mechanical hard disk is composed of components such as platters, read/write heads, a spindle motor, and a voice coil motor. The platters spin at high speeds (commonly 5400 or 7200 RPM), while the read/write head moves across the surface of the platters, driven by the voice coil motor, to perform data reading and writing operations.

**2.Main Sources of Noise:**

* **Seek Noise from Read/Write Head** (typical “clicking” sound): During random read/write operations, the head frequently jumps across tracks. The rapid start-stop movements of the voice coil motor produce mechanical impact sounds. The more fragmented the files, the more frequent the noise.
* **Spindle Vibration Noise** (low-frequency “buzzing” sound): The high-speed rotation of the platters can cause vibration due to imbalance. Models with 7200 RPM tend to be noisier than those with 5400 RPM.
* **Abnormal Contact Noise:** When the head returns to its parked position abnormally (e.g., during a sudden power-off or triggered power protection), or when platter wear occurs, sharp friction sounds may be heard—this requires immediate attention.
* **Example Scenario:** When performing disk defragmentation, the read/write head must scan the entire disk to reorganize data. This process results in a dense and rhythmic “clicking” sound pattern.

3.**Noise Characteristics of Solid-State Drives (SSD)**  SSDs use flash memory chips to store data, eliminating moving parts such as platters, read/write heads, and motors found in HDDs. As a result, there is no seek noise, no rotational vibration, and SSDs operate in complete silence—achieving true 0 dB noise levels.

### **II****、External Factors That Amplify Noise**

**1.Device Resonance**​ If the hard disk is not properly secured during installation—such as loose screws or an improperly fitted mounting bracket—the vibration generated by spinning platters can be transmitted through the bracket to the chassis, resulting in a “buzzing” resonance noise. Typical case: If the HDD is misaligned or not firmly fixed, the noise will be amplified.

**2.Influence of Hardware Specifications**

|  |  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Specofication** | 5400 RPM HDD | 7200 RPM HDD | Enterprise-grade 10K RPM HDD | SSD |
| **Typical Noise** | 30-40dB | 40-50dB | 50-60dB | 0-15dB |
| **Noise Characteristics** | Primarily low-frequency | Primarily mid-frequency | Noticeable high-frequency vibration | No mechanical noise |

* In multi-platter HDDs, higher capacities (e.g., above 8TB) involve more read/write heads. The increased number of heads leads to overlapping seek noises, resulting in higher overall noise levels.

**3.Gradual Impact of Aging and Wear**

* Bearing lubricant failure: Causes increased resistance in spindle rotation, often producing a continuous "squeaking" sound—an early sign of aging.
* Wear of actuator arm gears: Leads to seek positioning deviations, manifested as irregular "clicking" intervals, often accompanied by a rise in read/write error rates.

### III、Normal Noise Range and Abnormality Identification

#### **HDD Noise Evaluation**

**1.Normal Operating Noises (Within Acceptable Range)**

* **Low-frequency (at 1-meter distance):** A steady "humming" sound from platter rotation, usually only noticeable in very quiet environments (e.g., at night).
* **Mid-frequency (at 1-meter distance):**

  + Rhythmic "clicking" sounds from normal head seeking operations.
  + Initialization sounds during system startup (head calibration).
  + Continuous low-frequency vibration sounds during large file read/write operations.

**2.Abnormal Fault Indicators (Require Attention)**

* **Abnormal Sounds (at 1-meter distance):**

  + Sharp "clacking" sounds, possibly caused by the head touching the platter surface, which may result in physical damage.
  + Periodic loud banging noises, indicating the actuator arm may be crashing against the stopper due to loss of control.
* **Aging Warning Signs:**

  + High-pitched "sizzling" noises caused by bearing wear, leading to unstable spindle rotation.
  + Intermittent stuttering sounds from repeated retries when the head encounters bad sectors.

#### **SSD Noise Characteristics**

**1.Ideal Condition:** Completely silent—almost no audible sound even when close to the device.

**2.Abnormal Behavior:** High-frequency electrical noise (＞20 dB) under heavy load, possibly caused by inadequate power filtering of the controller chip. While it does not affect functionality, it is recommended to monitor the device’s thermal performance.

### IV、Noise Differences Among Different Types of Hard Disks

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dimension** | **Mechanical Hard Disk (HDD)** | **Solid State Drive (SSD)** |
| **Noise Nature** | Physical vibration noise generated by mechanical components | Electromagnetic noise from high-frequency operation of electronic components (rare) |
| **Load Dependency** | Noise becomes more noticeable with higher random read/write operations | No direct correlation between noise and load (no mechanical movement) |
| **Environmental Impact** | Significantly affected by temperature/vibration | Strong resistance to environmental interference |
| **Typical Scenarios** | Head reset sound during system startup | Inductive whine under extreme load (rare) |

### **V****、Summary and Recommendations**

* **Complete Silence Requirement:** Prioritize SSDs for a balance of speed and zero noise.
* **Scenario-based Usage:** Combine SSDs (for frequent read/write tasks) with HDDs (for tasks with less frequent data access), using the SSD to handle high-frequency read/write operations to reduce overall noise.
